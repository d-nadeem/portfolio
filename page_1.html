3D Octopus Arm End Effector

Problem Statement

The 2D Octopus Arm project revealed a key limitation: The design could not easily integrade 3 DOF. The 2D arm was limited to 1 DOF, bend on a plane. When the end effector is parallel to the ground, the design also required gravity to create an inward spiral to avoid self-collision. Expanding to 3 DOF would enable a larger range of mobility, which is particularly valuable for pick-and-place tasks. Prior research by Wang et al. (2024) attempted to address this limitation through a logarithmic spiral-based soft robot design, but it encountered similar constraints in its design. This gap highlights the need for a design approach that can reliably deliver 3 DOF while remaining practical to fabricate and control.

Project Scope

• Provide 3 degrees of freedom
• Used repetitive components to enable mass production and reduce costs
• Bill of materials capped at $100
• Explored pulley mechanisms for cost reduction, compactness, and design simplicity
• Explore controller UI
• Reduced item rejection rate due to defects
• End effector length: 800mm
• Switched material from TPU to PLA for added compressive strength
• Maximum assembly weight: 1.5kg
• Maximum rope tension: 18kg (X, Y, and Z axes)

Process

Process Description

Project is based on the research paper “https://www.cell.com/device/pdfExtended/S2666-9986(24)00603-3.” A major part of the project involved transitioning from a TPU-based design to a predominantly PLA-based system to improve print reliability, structural consistency, and ease of assembly. Throughout development, I used CAD modeling, iterative prototyping, to reduce the number of components needed for assembly.

Troubleshooting

• Design required too much support material
• Design was too complex to 3D print
• Exceeded the weight limit, per calculations
• Excessive friction between joints
• Redundant components in the design
• Threads locked between links during operation
• Threads could not withstand tension forces
• Grasp path had a wide curvature, preventing it from grasping intended items
• Required extensive clamping hardware

Result

I redesigned and optimized an octopus-inspired robotic arm by first analyzing the original mechanism to identify areas where component count, assembly complexity, and manufacturability could be improved. Using CAD software, I simplified the mechanical architecture and redesigned several structural components to create a more compact and practical system for 3D printing and assembly.
